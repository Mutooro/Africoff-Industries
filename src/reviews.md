For example, images are not consistently loading on the About Us page, including the Who We Are section. The site currently also mixes externally hosted images, such as the About hero image coming from Unsplash, with images hosted directly by Africof. I would recommend hosting critical organizational images directly on the website/CDN, optimizing them into WebP/AVIF, adding responsive image sizes and a fallback if an image fails. The site should also be tested systematically on Android phones, iPhones, tablets and desktop because most stakeholders in Uganda will not necessarily access it from the same device or connection. 
I would also review the gallery carefully. There are some very good authentic field photographs showing Africoff activities and farmer engagement, and these are much stronger than generic imagery. At the same time, the gallery appears to mix project photographs with more generic or stock imagery, including an asset whose filename identifies it as a Getty image. If that image is licensed, that is fine, but I would confirm the rights. More importantly, I would prioritize Africcof's own photographs because they are much more credible for an institutional organization. Each important image should ideally include the activity, location, date, project and partner rather than only a generic caption. 
 
The About Us page presents the operational team well, including Maate, Beatrice, David and the technical team, but I would add a governance section if Africoff has a Board, directors, advisory committee or other oversight structure. For institutional partners, understanding who governs the organization is different from knowing who works there. 
The Impact section needs substantial strengthening. The site currently reports 4,532+ farmers reached, 100+ beehives sited, 50,000+ trees planted and five operating locations. These are useful numbers, but a donor or institutional partner will immediately ask: as of what date, under which projects, how are farmers counted, are they unique farmers, how many are women/youth, how many are active, and how were the tree numbers verified? I would keep the numbers but add a measurement date, source/methodology and eventually downloadable annual or impact reports.

The Programs section is conceptually strong, but it should become much more concrete. At present the site identifies three major programs: Climate-Smart Agriculture, Farm-to-Market Enterprise Development and Community Well-Fair. However, the public/indexable version of the Programs page exposes detailed content primarily for Climate-Smart Agriculture. I would give each major program its own permanent page or URL, with objectives, geographic area, start/end dates, target beneficiaries, implementing partners, funding partners if applicable, activities, outputs, results and downloadable supporting material. Also, two of them, the last two are missing pictures. 
The Value Chains section also needs considerable expansion. Right now Coffee, Cocoa and Honey are each explained in only a few sentences. If this website is going to support market development, buyers need much more: production areas, farmer network, varieties, harvest periods, processing methods, quality controls, estimated volumes, traceability, available certifications, current capabilities, packaging/value addition, and a specific commercial contact. Ideally there should eventually be downloadable product or origin sheets. 
I would also consider adding Bwana coffee properly to the website. Africoff is already publicly describing Bwana coffee as a social enterprise initiative focused on domestic coffee processing, packaging, distribution and farmer market access, yet that story is much more visible on LinkedIn than on the main website. This is a good concrete example of Africoff moving from development activity into value-chain enterprise, and it deserves a proper section. LinkedIn
The News section should be converted into a proper publication system. Each story should have its own URL, title, date, author or Africoff attribution, photographs, location, related program and ideally a short results section. At the moment the homepage “Read More”  is not there, and when you click on each story, the links lead visitors back to the general News page, and the News page itself mainly presents short summaries. Individual articles are much more useful because they can be shared with a donor, referenced in a proposal, indexed by Google and used as evidence of program activity. 
I would also correct the ordering and copy editing of the News section. For example, May 6 and May 7 stories are not presented in chronological order, and there are several small English issues such as “three weeks long training,” “five year strategic plan,” “likeminded organisations,” and “emphasises on.” None of these are major individually, but consistent professional English matters when an international institution is evaluating the organization. 

There are also some structural items I would recommend for the website before using it heavily for international institutional engagement. I was not able to find publicly accessible sections for items such as a Privacy/Data Protection Policy, safeguarding or child-protection policy, complaints/whistleblowing mechanism, anti-fraud/anti-corruption policy, annual reports, strategic plan, governance documents or organizational policies. Not all of these need to be displayed prominently, but having an “Accountability / Documents / Resources” section makes a major difference when a donor or partner is doing due diligence.
SEO and accessibility should also be reviewed. Several important pages currently return the same page title, “Africof | Empowering Farmers Across Uganda,” instead of having unique titles such as “About Africof Uganda,” “Africof Impact,” or “Coffee, Cocoa and Honey Value Chains.” The current /products URL is also presented to users as “Value Chains,” so I would consider changing or redirecting it to /value-chains. Unique metadata, Open Graph images, structured Organization data, descriptive image alt text and accessible program tabs would improve both Google visibility and usability.
